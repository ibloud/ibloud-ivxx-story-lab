# The Signal Between Us

## Public treatment

**Format:** Feature film or five-part limited series  
**Genre:** Techno-noir music drama / transmedia mystery  
**Status:** Interpretive and fictionalized development material  
**Authorship:** Story world and source archive by Dominique Devereaux; structure and prose organized with OpenAI/ChatGPT assistance

### Logline

When an independent hacker tries to reconstruct a lost pitch from fragments scattered across the public web, she becomes entangled with the rapper at the center of the archive. To reclaim their work from a platform economy that monetizes confusion, they must build a trustworthy record without turning each other into content.

### The premise

IBLoud IVXX is not a hacker who steals secrets. She is a systems reader: someone who sees relationships among public posts, music cues, broken links, timestamps and algorithmic echoes. She believes a missing post once named the project clearly. Unable to find it, she follows the visible pattern instead.

The rapper is publicly recognizable but intentionally fictionalized in this treatment. His image travels farther and faster than his consent. Every appearance becomes a theory; every theory becomes a commodity. He initially assumes IBLoud is another spectator trying to possess his story.

Their collision reveals the antagonist: not a secret cabal, but an attention system that rewards certainty, imitation and emotional escalation while erasing source context.

### Central question

Can the hacker preserve the signal without claiming the person—and can the rapper trust an archive that began without his participation?

## Five-part structure

### Part I — Signal

A fleeting public artifact triggers an associative trail across Tumblr, Audio.com and music fragments. IBLoud remembers a pitch about a hacker and a rapper, but the post itself is missing.

**Turn:** She decides to publish the search method, not a claim that she found the truth.

### Part II — Noise

The network fills the gap. Reblogs, headlines and recommendation systems make speculation feel documented. The rapper sees the growing archive and reads it as surveillance.

**Turn:** IBLoud discovers that technically public information can still be framed unethically.

### Part III — Counter-signal

A contradiction breaks the apparent narrative. The hacker and rapper communicate indirectly through corrections, music and public boundaries rather than private access.

**Turn:** Both recognize that the system profits from keeping them in conflict.

### Part IV — The Lab

IBLoud rebuilds the project as an auditable story lab: every artifact receives a truth-status label; uncleared music is replaced or quarantined; human and AI contributions are recorded; a correction path is opened.

**Turn:** The rapper's choice is not whether to validate her interpretation. It is whether, and on what terms, he wants to participate in a new fictional work.

### Part V — Return

A present-day tour renews public attention. The pair use the moment to test a different model: official ticket channels remain official; the lab measures discovery without claiming causation; contributors are credited; no private story is sold as fact.

**Resolution:** The lost pitch remains unresolved. The ethical method becomes the work.

## Character functions

| Character | Wants | Fears | Ethical movement |
|---|---|---|---|
| IBLoud IVXX | Make the pattern legible | That deletion will erase meaning | From reconstruction-as-certainty to preservation-with-consent |
| The Rapper | Retain agency over public identity | That every boundary becomes more content | From defensive distance to conditional collaboration |
| The Network | Convert attention into repeat engagement | Context, correction and accountable ownership | It does not reform; the creators build an alternative practice |
| The Audience | Solve the mystery | Being excluded from the “real” story | From spectatorship to evidence-aware participation |

## Public-consumption boundaries

- The rapper is a fictional dramatic role unless a named person expressly agrees to participate.
- No hacking, unauthorized access, private communications or nonpublic personal information are claimed.
- Public figures and events may be discussed only with status labels and citations.
- Fictional scenes must not be presented as reenactments of verified private events.
- A named artist's music, likeness, trademarks or footage require separate review and, where applicable, written permission.
- “Inspired by” does not erase clearance, publicity, endorsement or defamation risk.
