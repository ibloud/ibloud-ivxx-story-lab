**Format explored:** Five-part limited series or contained feature film

> This is a portfolio case study, not an investment solicitation or an application to raise money on Republic. Republic has not reviewed, commissioned, endorsed or participated in this project.

## The challenge

IBLoud IVXX existed across music experiments, Tumblr archives, public reporting, platform memory and a wider Loptr Lab/GerryLand creative practice. The material carried a recognizable emotional and narrative pattern, but it was distributed across sources with different ownership, reliability and legal status.

The task was not simply to collect everything or turn public fragments into biography. The challenge was to answer four questions:

1. Is there a coherent original story inside the archive?
2. Can it become legible to producers, collaborators and rights reviewers?
3. Can the process distinguish documented evidence from interpretation and fiction?
4. Can the project invite participation without implying that a named artist has already participated?

## The central design decision

The remembered “hacker and rapper” pitch could not be located as a verified Tumblr post. I chose not to disguise that absence.

Instead, the repository records the missing pitch as **UNRESOLVED** and labels the resulting treatment **INTERPRETIVE + FICTIONALIZED**. That decision changed the story itself. The hacker does not break into a celebrity's private life or prove a theory about him. She discovers that access to public information is not permission, memory is not evidence, and an archive can reproduce the same loss of agency it claims to oppose.

The limitation became the dramatic engine:

> Can two creators recover agency from a system that profits when audiences confuse public signals with private truth?

## How I approached the work

### 1. Make the archive inspectable

I organized the project so another person could trace its claims instead of being asked to trust a finished narrative. The repository separates source notes, interpretation, fictional scenes, market experiments and legal-development questions.

### 2. Design truth status into the story

The project uses five labels:

- **DOCUMENTED** — preserved evidence with creator, date and source;
- **ATTRIBUTED** — a public statement accurately credited;
- **INTERPRETIVE** — analysis connecting artifacts;
- **FICTIONALIZED** — invented character action, scene or dialogue; and
- **UNRESOLVED** — remembered or incomplete material that has not been verified.

These are editorial controls and story mechanics. They allow mystery without presenting uncertainty as fact.

### 3. Treat rights as architecture

Music, story, performance, name/likeness, artwork, footage, trademarks, platform terms and AI assistance are separate rights layers. The clearance ledger uses **UNKNOWN**, **REFERENCE ONLY** and **CLEARED** as operational states.

A public link is not treated as permission. A fair-use rationale is not treated as a license. A revenue share is not treated as ownership.

### 4. Separate the fictional role from a living artist

The Rapper is written as an original dramatic role unless a named person expressly chooses to participate under written terms. The screenplay does not assign private statements, criminal conduct, relationships or endorsement to a living person.

That boundary preserves the possibility of an authorized collaboration without making authorization a prerequisite for evaluating the original premise.

### 5. Build a pathway from concept to participation

The package does not end with “here is my idea.” It states what kind of evaluation is requested, what materials are ready, what remains uncleared and what the parties would need to define before development begins.

## Problems solved during production

### A story without a recoverable source post

Rather than manufacturing provenance, I documented the failed search and preserved the treatment as a dated interpretation. If the source is later found, it can be added without silently rewriting the history.

### A public figure inside an original story

I separated cultural research from character ownership. Public events and named artists remain references; the fictional role, scenes and dialogue do not claim private knowledge or participation.

### Music that functions creatively but is not uniformly clearable

The soundtrack map preserves what each development track taught the story while quarantining third-party mashups and unknown assets from commercial use. It also proposes an original six-cue replacement suite.

### AI assistance inside a human-led project

The repository documents what I supplied and decided, what AI assisted with, what records should be retained and why living-artist imitation is outside the workflow.

### A financial idea that could be mistaken for a promise

The waterfall is presented as an attorney-review development model. It separates project receipts from catalogue royalties, master and publishing income, tour revenue and other third-party income.

## Outcome

The result is not a finished film and does not pretend to be one. It is a development system that makes the project evaluable.

A recipient can now:

- understand the premise in one page;
- read an original opening sample;
- inspect the full treatment and episode engine;
- see how music functions and what is not cleared;
- trace the project's sources and uncertainties;
- evaluate the proposed legal and financial architecture; and
- respond through a bounded collaboration path.

That is the outcome I was trying to prove: nonlinear creative practice can become a disciplined, inspectable package without erasing how it was made.

## What I would do next

1. Tailor the package to the exact recipient and decision being requested.
2. Develop the opening sample into a pilot or feature screenplay.
3. Replace reference-only tracks with original, documented cues.
4. Complete creator-controlled Tumblr exports and private chain-of-title records.
5. Obtain qualified entertainment-law review before using a named-person version, raising money or distributing third-party material.
6. Build a budget, schedule and financing plan only after the creative format and rights path are defined.
7. Test the public story with privacy-respecting measurements and record what audiences actually do rather than claiming impact.
